When your phone screen cracks and the manufacturer says replacing it voids your warranty, that is not a technical limitation. It is a business decision. The right to repair movement is pushing back.

### The surface argument

Consumers should be able to fix things they own. Manufacturers should provide parts, tools, and documentation. This seems obvious and yet most electronics are designed to prevent exactly this.

### The deeper argument

Repairability is a design philosophy. A device designed to be repaired is designed to last. A device designed to be replaced is designed to generate revenue. These are opposing goals and they produce opposing kinds of objects.

When you extend the life of a laptop from three years to seven, you have halved its environmental impact without changing anything about how it was manufactured. Repair is the most effective form of recycling.

### What this means for software

The same logic applies to software. Open source is repairability for code. Self-hosting is repairability for services. When a company shuts down a product you depend on, the ability to repair — to fork, to migrate, to run your own — is the difference between continuity and loss.

The right to repair is not just about hardware. It is about who controls the things we depend on.
