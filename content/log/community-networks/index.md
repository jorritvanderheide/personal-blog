In Barcelona, Guifi.net operates one of the largest community networks in the world. Over 35,000 nodes, built and maintained by the people who use them. No ISP. No monthly bill. Just rooftop antennas and fiber runs organized by neighbors.

<!--more-->

### How it works

Someone installs an antenna on their roof. They connect to a nearby node. They become part of the mesh. Traffic routes through the network without touching the commercial internet unless it needs to. Local services — file sharing, video calls, websites — stay local.

The hardware is commodity: cheap routers, directional antennas, some fiber. The hard part is social, not technical. You need people who will climb roofs, run cables, and show up to meetings.

### Why this matters

A community network is infrastructure owned by its users. It cannot be sold to a private equity firm. It cannot decide to throttle your traffic. It cannot harvest your browsing data. The incentive structure is fundamentally different from commercial internet provision.

It also works when the commercial internet does not. In disasters, community mesh networks have provided the only connectivity. They are resilient by design because they have no single point of failure.

### The connection to low-tech

Community networks and permacomputing share a principle: technology should be maintained by the people who depend on it. If your internet connection requires a multinational corporation to function, you do not really control it. If your neighbor can fix it from their roof, you do.
