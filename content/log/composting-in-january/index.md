People assume composting is a summer activity. The pile slows down in winter but it does not stop.

<!--more-->

The microbial activity that breaks down organic matter drops as temperatures fall, but it never reaches zero. A well-insulated pile — straw bales around the sides, a tarp on top — maintains enough internal heat to keep decomposing through a Dutch winter.

The key is carbon. In summer the challenge is too much nitrogen (food scraps, green waste). In winter the balance shifts because you are adding fewer greens. Keep adding shredded cardboard, dry leaves, and straw. The browns insulate and feed the slower winter microbes.

By spring the pile has settled and partially broken down. It is not finished compost yet but it is halfway there, and you did not have to do anything except keep feeding it.

Small processes that continue through unfavorable conditions. There is something in that.
